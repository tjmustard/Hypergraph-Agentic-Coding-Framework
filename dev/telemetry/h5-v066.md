## The builder verdict (R72)

Rule: held when non-mixed n is at least 3, audit FAILs per slice are at most B2's, and escapes per
slice are at most B2's; not held when either exceeds B2's; not judged when n is under 3.

Computed: non-mixed n = 8; FAILs per slice 0.000 against B2's 0.100; escapes per slice 0.000 against
B2's 0.200. **build-execute: held.**

Three things the numbers do not say by themselves, stated so the reader can weigh the verdict:

1. **Slice .1 is not counted, and it had audit FAILs.** Its audit took three iterations (two FAILs,
   both bookkeeping: a proof without frontmatter, then `executing`/`executed` not recorded; the
   contract passed from iteration 1). The collector counts one (from the slice's progress record);
   the audit's loop record shows two. Counted anyway, FAILs per slice would be 1/9 = 0.111 (or
   2/9 = 0.222 by the loop record) against B2's 0.100, which is a `not held` figure. The rule
   excluded mixed slices before the result was known, and this note follows it.
2. **The collector does not see every stop.** The builder stopped to ask the lead four times, each
   because an existing test outside the slice's file list asserted behaviour the slice changes or a
   path in the spec was wrong: slices .3 (P36), .4 (P37), .9 (P38) and .6 (P39), rows from
   build-execute to orch-lead at 01:32Z, 01:57Z, 02:29Z and 03:12Z. Each was a spec defect the
   builder found, each was answered by a mechanical replan the same hour, and the builder carried on
   rather than halting, so no `blocked` event exists and the collector counts none. B2 recorded two
   builder halts in 20 slices. If these four were counted as halts, escapes per slice would be
   4/8 = 0.500 against B2's 0.200 (`not held`). The rule's own instrument counts none, and the
   verdict follows the instrument.
3. **`blocked` is unknown for .2, .3 and .4** (no record that halt tracking was on); the escape
   figure above is the sum of the known parts. The queue shows no `blocked` row in the window.

This verdict is about the seat that built this slice; the Opus audit re-derives the numbers.
